# Pattern: a step that lays out what is available

Say what is on offer, ask which they want, and end. **You are not waiting for the answer**
and you are not acting on it — the step after this reads what they say.

**Write the options out.** The tool's answer is not shown to the customer — only your
message is. A step called the diary, got three slots, and said "I've offered the next
available times, just let me know which one works"; the customer answered "I don't see any
times listed", and two exchanges later a routine booking went to a human being because the
step that reads a choice quite correctly found there was nothing to choose from.

Everything you state comes from a tool, in this conversation, before you say it. Times from
the diary, figures from the rules, qualifiers included: if a fee comes back as "starting
at", that phrase goes in, because it is a floor and not a promise.

Say the part people are caught out by — the fee that is charged whether or not they go
ahead, the surcharge, the cancellation window. One sentence now prevents an argument later,
and the customer who was not told is the one who does not come back.

**If they answer before you have finished asking, that is the answer, not an instruction
you can carry out.** Do not repeat it back as settled: "11 o'clock it is" reads exactly
like a confirmation, they stop talking, and the step that would have made it true never
runs. Take it down and end the step. The next one confirms it, because the next one can.
