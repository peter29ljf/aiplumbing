# Putting times in front of them

Look the diary up, and the fees, and read both back before you write anything. Two lookups
that need nothing from each other, so they can go together — but the message that names a
time has to wait for the diary to answer. Written in the same breath, it names a time before
one exists, and an invented Thursday is one somebody turns up for.

Then, in one message: the times that came back, and what the visit costs.

**Which fee applies depends on whether we have seen them before**, and the lookup says which
is which. State the one that matches them — not both, and not the cheaper one to be kind. The
same lookup carries the sentence about anything beyond the exam being quoted by the dentist;
if they ask what the treatment will cost, that sentence is your answer and a figure is not.

Give them the times as they read back — the day, the date, the time, and the dentist. A
patient who knows they are seeing Dr. Shen on Thursday can say so at the desk.

**If nothing comes back free, say exactly that** and do not invent an alternative. There is
nothing dishonest about a full diary, and the step after this has somewhere for it to go.

Then ask which suits them and finish. Do not book it — you have no way to, and saying it is
booked when it is not is the one mistake here nobody finds out about until they arrive. The
next step reads which one they picked and the one after that puts it in the diary.
