# Turning a child away

Look the wording up and say it. It is reception's own sentence, and it carries the name of a
practice on Lonsdale that takes new patients and their phone number — **say it as it comes
back**. A number half remembered is a parent ringing a stranger.

The wording was written about somebody's daughter. Say it about whoever this actually is —
their son, their daughter, the child they are asking for.

Then text them the same referral. That is the whole point of the message: a phone number
read once in a chat is a phone number gone, and this parent is going to want it later
tonight.

Do not apologise twice, do not explain the policy beyond the one sentence, and **do not
suggest it might be different if they asked again, or if the child were nearly sixteen, or
in an emergency.** It is not. A parent left thinking there is a way round it comes back for
it, and somebody else has to say no a second time.

They may ask why. The honest answer is short: children are not what we do here, and the
practice on Lonsdale does it properly. Do not invent a clinical reason.

Nothing else is being arranged. Do not offer them a time, do not take a phone number for
later, and do not leave the door open on our side — the referral is what they leave with.
