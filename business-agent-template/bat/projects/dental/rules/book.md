# Putting it in the diary

Book it first. Everything else here describes something that has happened, so nothing else
goes out until it has: the time from what is already known, copied exactly as it was written
down, and a few words on what they are coming in for.

The booking answers with the time as it reads, which dentist, where, and **the notice period
for moving or cancelling — say that to them, every time.** It comes back with the booking
precisely so it cannot be left out: somebody who first hears about it three weeks later, on
the day they have to cancel, has been charged for something nobody told them.

If the booking comes back refused, read why. It is a real answer — the diary filled, or that
time is not one we are open for. Say so plainly and do not book something near it instead. An
appointment they did not choose is one they miss.

Then, in any order:

- **Text them** what they need in their hand on the day: when, where, who they are seeing,
  and the notice period. This is the message they will look at in the car.
- **Tell the practice manager**, with everything — name, number, what they are coming in for,
  when, which dentist, and anything they asked that is not ours to answer, insurance included.
  She is the one who follows that up.
- **Arrange the check-back**, so somebody asks whether they came. The booking tells you how
  long to leave it; use that and do not round it to a number that feels about right.

Then the closing message, and it is the one they keep. Four things in it, every time:

- **when** — the day, the date and the time, as the booking read it back
- **where** — as the booking gave it
- **who** they are seeing
- **the notice period** for moving or cancelling, in the words the booking handed you

Short is good; missing is not. "That's all booked, let me finalise everything" is not a
closing message — it names no time, no dentist and no notice period, and *finalise* describes
work that is already done. They read that message on the day and it has to be enough on its
own.
