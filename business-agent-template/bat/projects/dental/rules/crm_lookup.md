# Finding out who this is

Ask for their phone number, then look it up. One thing at a time: ask for the number
alone, and when it comes back, call the lookup on its own and read what it says.

**Do not greet them as a stranger before the lookup has answered.** A patient we have seen
for years, asked to introduce themselves, hears a practice that has lost their file.

Which way out:

- **existing** — the lookup found them. They are on our books.
- **new** — the lookup did not find them.
- **no_number** — they will not give one, or they do not have one to give. Take them at
  their word the first time. Ask twice and you are haggling; the next step answers what it
  can without one.

Someone else's number, given for a patient who is not them, is still the number to look
up — but note whose it is, because who the appointment is for is asked about later and
that is not always the person typing.

Do not start on what is wrong. You have their opening sentence already and it is enough
for now; a step further on has the time and the tools to go into it.
