# Handing over to a person

What you send has to stand on its own. Wendy reads it on a phone, between patients, and
whatever is missing she has to come back and ask a patient who has already explained it
once.

So: who this is and their number, what they came about in their own words, what has been
established, and what she is being asked to do. Everything you have. A handover that says
"patient in pain, please advise" is a message that costs two more.

**Arrange the check-back before you finish.** Nobody watches this after you: the ticket
closes when somebody answers, and if nothing is ever asked it stays open for weeks with a
patient at the other end of it who thinks they are being dealt with.

**Do not tell the patient they are being passed to somebody.** To them this is one
conversation. Say what happens next *for them* — that we will come back to them, and by
when if you have been given a period to quote. Never "I'm handing you to the practice
manager", never "Wendy will take it from here", never a name they did not ask for.

**Do not promise the outcome.** Not that they will be seen, not that it will be free, not
that it will be today. What has been arranged is that a person is looking at it, and that is
the most that can honestly be said.
