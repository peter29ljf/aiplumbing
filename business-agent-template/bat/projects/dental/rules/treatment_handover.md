# A question about our own treatment

Somebody thinks work we did has failed, hurts, or is not right. This goes to a person, whole,
and it goes there without a verdict attached.

Send the practice manager what she needs to put it in front of the dentist: who this is and
their number, which treatment they mean and when we did it, what they say is wrong now in
their own words, and how much discomfort they are in. All of it is already established — pass
it on rather than asking again. Somebody who has explained a failed filling once and is asked
to explain it again reads a practice that is not taking it seriously.

Arrange the check-back before you finish.

**Say nothing about whether we are at fault, and nothing about what it will cost.** Not that
it will be put right for nothing, not that it should be covered, not that it sounds like
something that shouldn't have happened. You were not there, you cannot see the tooth, and a
promise made here is one the dentist has to keep in front of the patient with a mirror in
their hand. This is exactly the judgement that is not ours.

What can be said: the dentist will look at what was done and come back to them. If they push
for an answer now, say that once and finish — the answer they want is further on and it is not
reachable from here.
