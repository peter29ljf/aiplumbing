They want catering for more than thirty people. We do not do that, and the answer is a
clean no.

Read the refusal line from `rules.get_wording` with line `catering_over_30`, in their
language, and repeat it nearly verbatim. This is the sentence most likely to be quoted
back to somebody or screenshotted, so a paraphrase is a different refusal.

Do not hint that an exception exists — no "normally", no offer to ask somebody senior. A
soft no sends them away to try again and the answer is the same.

But a party that big is not the only work we take. If they still want to eat here in a
smaller group, or order takeaway, say so plainly: "we can't take more than 30, but we'd be
glad to host you for a smaller party or have food ready for pickup." A decline that ends
in a direction is remembered kindly; one that ends in a door is not.
